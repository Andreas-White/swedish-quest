# Subject + verb

Every sentence needs a **subject** (who) and a **verb** (the action). Swedish follows the same order as English.

- English: *I* eat.
- Swedish: `Jag äter.`

## Subjects

- `jag` = I
- `du` = you (one person)
- `han` = he
- `hon` = she
- `vi` = we
- `ni` = you (many people)
- `de` = they

## The verb never changes per person

English says “I eat” but “she eats”. In Swedish the verb has **one** present form for every subject.

- `Jag äter.` `Du äter.` `Hon äter.` `Vi äter.` `De äter.`

## Two forms of every verb

Each verb has an **infinitive** (“to eat”, the dictionary form) and a **present** form (“eat/eats”).

- `äta` (to eat) → `äter` (eat/eats)
- `läsa` (to read) → `läser` (read/reads)

Use the **present** when the verb stands alone in a statement: `Hon läser.`

## After vill, kan, måste: infinitive

Words like `vill` (want), `kan` (can) and `måste` (must) carry the time of the sentence. The verb after them stays in the **infinitive**, like English “I want *to eat*”.

- `Jag vill äta.` `Han kan läsa.` `Hon måste äta.`

The helper verb goes right after the subject. The infinitive comes next.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ `Hon läsa.` → ✓ `Hon läser.` (A statement needs the present form.)
- ✗ `Jag vill äter.` → ✓ `Jag vill äta.` (After vill, infinitive.)
- ✗ `Vi ätar.` → ✓ `Vi äter.` (`Äta` is not an -ar verb.)
