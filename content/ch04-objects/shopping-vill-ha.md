# Shopping with vill ha

In a shop, English "I want a sandwich" becomes Swedish "I want *to have*". Always add `ha`.

- `Jag vill ha en smörgås.` (I want a sandwich.)
- `Jag vill ha ett äpple.` (I want an apple.)
- `Jag vill ha kaffe.` (I want coffee.)

Dropping `ha` is the number one shopping mistake.

## Match en and ett

The article must match the noun.

- `Jag vill ha en bok.` (I want a book.)
- `Jag vill ha en bil.` (Pointing in a toy shop — I want a car.)
- `Jag vill ha ett hus.` (Joking — I want a house.)
- `Jag vill ha en tidning.` (I want a newspaper.)

Keep the article with the noun: `en bok`, `ett hus`.

## Ask the price

- `Hur mycket kostar den?` (How much does it cost? — en-word.)
- `Hur mycket kostar det?` (How much does it cost? — ett-word.)
- `Hur mycket kostar boken?` (How much does the book cost?)

Short version works everywhere:

- `Hur mycket kostar den här?` (How much is this one?)

## Pay and thank

- `En kaffe, tack.` (One coffee, please.)
- `Ett äpple, tack.` (One apple, please.)
- `Tack! Hej då!` (Thanks! Goodbye!)

`Tack` at the end makes any request polite.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ `Jag vill en smörgås.` → ✓ `Jag vill ha en smörgås.` (Shopping needs `vill ha`.)
- ✗ `Jag vill ha ett bok.` → ✓ `Jag vill ha en bok.` (`Bok` is an en-word.)
- ✗ `Hur mycket kostar?` → ✓ `Hur mycket kostar den?` (Add `den` or the item.)

## Summary

- Want something: `Jag vill ha …`
- Match gender: `en bok`, `ett äpple`.
- Price: `Hur mycket kostar den?`
- Polite close: `tack`.
