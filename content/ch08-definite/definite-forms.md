# Definite Forms

English says the book with a separate word. Swedish glues the ending onto the noun: `-en` for `en` words and `-et` for `ett` words.

- `en bok` → `boken`
- `ett hus` → `huset`

## En-words take -en

Add `-en` to the indefinite form. If the word already ends in a vowel, just add `-n`.

- `en bok` → `boken`
- `en bil` → `bilen`
- `en flicka` → `flickan`
- `Boken är bra.`

## Ett-words take -et

Add `-et` to the indefinite form. Words ending in a vowel just add `-t`.

- `ett hus` → `huset`
- `ett äpple` → `äpplet`
- `ett rum` → `rummet`
- `Huset är stort.`

## Indefinite vs definite

Use the short form the first time you mention something, and the long form after that.

- `Jag ser en bil.` → `Bilen är röd.`
- `Hon har ett hus.` → `Huset är stort.`

## Common mistakes

- ✗ `the bok` → ✓ `boken.` (Swedish uses an ending, not a separate word.)
- ✗ `en boken` → ✓ `boken.` (Never keep `en` with the definite form.)
- ✗ `ett huset` → ✓ `huset.` (Never keep `ett` with the definite form.)

## Summary

- `en` words: add `-en`: `boken`, `bilen`.
- `ett` words: add `-et`: `huset`, `äpplet`.
- Never combine `en`/`ett` with the definite ending.
- First mention indefinite, then definite.
