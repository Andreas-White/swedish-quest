# Subordinate clauses

A Swedish sentence can have two parts: a **main clause** (huvudsats) that can stand alone, and a **subordinate clause** (bisats) that cannot. The bisats adds detail and starts with a word like `att` or `som`.

- `Jag stannar hemma.` (main clause, complete alone)
- `Jag stannar hemma.` + `eftersom jag är sjuk.` (main + subordinate)

## Main vs subordinate

A main clause makes sense on its own. A subordinate clause does not — it leans on the main clause.

- `Hon jobbar idag.` (complete thought)
- `eftersom hon jobbar idag.` (incomplete — why? what happens?)
- `Vi ses imorgon.` + `om du har tid.`

Think: if you can put a period after it and it sounds finished, it is a main clause.

## Clauses started with att

`att` means "that". It introduces what someone says, thinks, or knows.

- `Jag tror att han kommer.`
- `Hon säger att hon är trött.`
- `Vi vet att tåget är sent.`
- `Han hoppas att det regnar inte.` (word order covered in bisats-order; note `inte` placement)

After verbs like `tro`, `säga`, `veta` and `hoppas`, use `att` before the next clause.

## Clauses started with som

`som` means "who / which / that". It describes a person or thing.

- `Boken som ligger här.` (the book that lies here)
- `Mannen som jobbar där.` (the man who works there)
- `Huset som vi köpte.` (the house that we bought)

`som` points back to a noun just before it.

## Comma rules, light

In modern Swedish the comma before `att` and `som` is usually **optional**. Keep it simple:

- No comma needed in short sentences: `Jag tror att han sover.`
- A comma is fine before a long bisats: `Han säger, att han kommer imorgon kväll.`
- Never put a comma between `som` and its verb: `Mannen som jobbar här.`

When in doubt, leave the comma out.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ `Eftersom hon jobbar idag.` → ✓ `Jag stannar hemma eftersom hon jobbar idag.` (A bisats cannot stand alone.)
- ✗ `Jag tror han kommer.` → ✓ `Jag tror att han kommer.` (Include `att` as a beginner.)
- ✗ `Boken, som ligger här.` → ✓ `Boken som ligger här.` (No comma needed in short clauses.)

## Summary

- A main clause stands alone; a subordinate clause needs a main clause.
- `att` introduces what someone says, thinks, or knows.
- `som` describes a person or thing (who / which / that).
- Commas before `att` and `som` are optional in short sentences.
