# Du and ni

Since the 1960s, Swedes say `du` to almost everyone. This reform is called the `du`-reform. `ni` survives as plural and in rare formal use.

- `Hur mår du?`
- `Vad heter du?`

## Du is always safe

Use `du` with friends, bosses, teachers and strangers. First names go with it.

- `Hej Anna, hur mår du?`
- `Tack, det är bra!`
- `Kan du hjälpa mig?`

## Ni as plural

`ni` is normal and neutral when you address several people.

- `Vad heter ni?`
- `Var bor ni någonstans?`
- `Kan ni komma imorgon?`

A singular formal `ni` exists but is rare, mostly with very elderly people. If someone says `ni` to you alone, just answer politely with `du` unless they clearly prefer `ni`.

## Skip herr and fru

Old titles like `herr` (Mr) and `fru` (Mrs) sound stiff today. Use first names instead.

- `Hej Anna!` (natural)
- `Hej Eva!` (natural)

In very formal letters you may still see `Bäste herr Lund`, but in speech you can skip it.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ `Hej fru Anna!` → ✓ `Hej Anna!` (Skip titles in speech.)
- ✗ `Vad heter ni?` (to one friend) → ✓ `Vad heter du?` (`ni` to one friend sounds odd.)
- ✗ Avoiding `du` with a boss from politeness → ✓ `Kan du hjälpa mig?` (`du` is polite in Sweden.)

## Summary

- `du` works with everyone since the 1960s.
- `ni` is plural: `Vad heter ni?`
- Avoid `herr` and `fru`; use first names.
