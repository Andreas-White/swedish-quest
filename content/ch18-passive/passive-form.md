# Passive form

Active says **who** does it: `Hon lagar maten.` Passive says **what happens**: `Maten lagas.` Swedish forms this easily by adding `-s` to the verb.

- `Hon stänger dörren.` (active)
- `Dörren stängs.` (passive)

## Add -s to the present

Take the present form and add `-s`. That is the s-passive.

- `lagar` → `lagas` (`Maten lagas.`)
- `stänger` → `stängs` (`Affären stängs.`)
- `tvättar` → `tvättas` (`Bilen tvättas.`)
- `gör` → `görs` (`Det görs här.`)

If the present already ends in `-s` (like `läsa` → `läser`), just add `-s` anyway: `läses`.

## What it means

The s-passive hides or generalises who acts. Use it for processes, signs, and routines.

- `Maten lagas varje dag.`
- `Dörren stängs klockan sex.`
- `Biljetter säljs här.`
- `Det talas svenska här.`

English would say "is cooked", "is closed", "are sold", "is spoken".

## Present only for now

At this level, use the s-passive in the **present**. Past passive forms come later.

- `Affären stängs nu.` (present, correct here)
- `Bilen tvättas idag.`
- `Maten lagas här.`

## Common mistakes

- ✗ `Maten lagar.` → ✓ `Maten lagas.` (Passive needs the `-s` ending.)
- ✗ `Dörren stänger.` → ✓ `Dörren stängs.` (`Stänger` is active — someone closes it.)
- ✗ `Det göra här.` → ✓ `Det görs här.` (Start from the present `gör`, then add `-s`.)

## Summary

- Active focuses on who; passive focuses on what happens.
- Add `-s` to the present: `lagar` → `lagas`.
- Use it for routines and signs: `Biljetter säljs här.`
- Stick to the present tense for now.
