# Pronouns and you

Every Swedish sentence starts with who. Learn these seven words first.

- `jag` (I)
- `du` (you — one person)
- `han` (he)
- `hon` (she)
- `vi` (we)
- `ni` (you — many people)
- `de` (they)

Use them with a verb: `Jag talar.` `Du talar.` `De talar.`

## Du for almost everyone

Since the 1960s, Sweden uses `du` to almost everyone: friends, teachers, bosses, strangers.

- `Du talar svenska.` (You speak Swedish.)
- `Bor du här?` (Do you live here?)

`Ni` means "you" to a group of people.

- `Bor ni här?` (Do you all live here?)

You will sometimes hear `ni` used to one stranger as extra formal. It is rare. For this course, `du` to one person is always right.

## Han, hon, de

- `Han bor här.` (He lives here.)
- `Hon bor här.` (She lives here.)
- `De bor här.` (They live here.)

`De` means they. It sounds like "dom" when spoken, but always spell it `de`.

`Vi` means we.

- `Vi bor här.` (We live here.)

## Common mistakes

- ✗ `Ni talar svenska.` (to one friend) → ✓ `Du talar svenska.` (`Ni` is for groups.)
- ✗ `Dom bor här.` → ✓ `De bor här.` (Spoken "dom", written `de`.)
- ✗ `Jag heter du Anna.` → ✓ `Jag heter Anna.` (No pronoun after `heter` here.)

## Summary

- `jag`, `du`, `han`, `hon`, `vi`, `ni`, `de`.
- `du` to one person, `ni` to many.
- `de` means they, spelled `de`.
