# Regular -ar verbs

Most Swedish verbs are regular "-ar" verbs. Learn one rule and you get hundreds of verbs.

Take the infinitive (the dictionary form) and add `r`.

- `tala` (to speak) → `talar` (speak / speaks)
- `stanna` (to stay) → `stannar` (stay / stays)
- `jobba` (to work) → `jobbar` (work / works)
- `fråga` (to ask) → `frågar` (ask / asks)
- `kalla` (to call) → `kallar` (call / calls)

One present form works for every person: `Jag talar.` `Hon talar.` `De talar.`

## Use it in sentences

- `Jag talar svenska.` (I speak Swedish.)
- `Du jobbar här.` (You work here.)
- `Vi stannar hemma.` (We stay home.)
- `Han frågar Erik.` (He asks Erik.)

The verb goes second, right after the subject. The rest comes after.

## More useful -ar verbs

- `bo` is short but works the same way: add `r`.
- `Jag bor i Sverige.` (I live in Sweden.)
- `Hon bor i Malmö.` (She lives in Malmö.)

Other friends:

- `prata` → `pratar` (talk)
- `lyssna` → `lyssnar` (listen)
- `titta` → `tittar` (look / watch)

Try: `Jag lyssnar.` `Du tittar.` `Vi pratar.`

## Common mistakes

- ✗ `Jag tala svenska.` → ✓ `Jag talar svenska.` (Statements need the present, with `r`.)
- ✗ `Hon talarar svenska.` → ✓ `Hon talar svenska.` (Add only one `r`.)
- ✗ `De jobba här.` → ✓ `De jobbar här.` (Same form for they.)

## Summary

- Infinitive plus `r` gives the present.
- `tala` → `talar`, `jobba` → `jobbar`.
- One form for `jag`, `du`, `han`, `hon`, `vi`, `ni`, `de`.
